# 01 — Daily Founder Workflow

**Audience:** Founder / CEO of a multi-initiative company (~$1M–$50M ARR)  
**Product:** Business Operating System (BOS)  
**Purpose:** Define the rhythm BOS should support—not the screens that render it.

---

## Design Principle

A founder does not open BOS to *use software*. They open it to **reduce uncertainty before the day steals their attention**.

The perfect workflow follows one rule:

> **Morning = orientation. Day = execution. Evening = closure. Week+ = judgment.**

BOS should never ask the founder to do bookkeeping in the morning or strategy at midnight.

---

## Morning (5–15 minutes)

**Intent:** Answer *“What matters today, and what is already going wrong?”*

### What the founder should see first

1. **Portfolio pulse** — How many initiatives are active, paused, or waiting on a decision?
2. **Attention queue** — The 1–3 initiatives that need founder action *today* (not all initiatives).
3. **Overnight signal** — Only material changes: budget threshold crossed, initiative stalled, decision overdue, unexpected spend attributed.
4. **Today’s decision surface** — One sentence per pending choice: *“Scale Meta spend on Digikinz?”*

### What the founder should do

| Action | Why |
|--------|-----|
| Scan the attention queue | Prevents reactive firefighting later |
| Confirm or defer 0–2 decisions | Keeps decision debt from compounding |
| Check one initiative health line | Not a dashboard tour—one bet they care about most |
| Ignore everything else | Trust that BOS filtered noise |

### What should *not* happen in the morning

- Creating ventures or initiatives from scratch (strategy work, not daily rhythm)
- Browsing expense lists or CRM pipelines (operational layer)
- Reading full decision histories (evening or weekly activity)
- Configuring permissions, categories, or integrations

### Morning success state

The founder closes BOS knowing:

- Which initiative gets their focus today
- Whether anything is silently burning cash without progress
- Whether they owe anyone a decision before noon

---

## Afternoon (2–5 minutes, optional)

**Intent:** Answer *“Is today’s bet still valid?”*

Most founders skip this unless BOS surfaces a trigger. That is correct behavior.

### When BOS should pull the founder back

- A decision they made this morning has new contradicting evidence (e.g., spend spike, pipeline stall)
- An initiative crossed a pre-agreed checkpoint (30-day review, budget 80%, zero leads in 14 days)
- A team member recorded a decision that requires founder ratification

### What the founder should do

- **Approve, amend, or pause**—never “review a page”
- Leave a one-line rationale if they override a recommendation (feeds institutional memory)

### Afternoon success state

No initiative is running on a decision the founder would revoke if they knew the latest facts.

---

## Evening (5–10 minutes)

**Intent:** Answer *“What did we learn today, and what closes the loop?”*

### What the founder should do

| Action | Why |
|--------|-----|
| Log 0–1 decisions made outside BOS | Captures reality; meetings still happen |
| Mark one initiative “progressed” or “blocked” | Lightweight status without project-management overhead |
| Close micro-loops | Approve attributions, confirm spend belongs to the right bet |
| Preview tomorrow’s attention queue | Reduces morning anxiety |

### What BOS should never require in the evening

- Full initiative close-out (weekly/monthly activity)
- Lesson-writing unless an initiative actually ended or pivoted
- Data entry for its own sake

### Evening success state

The founder feels **closure**: today’s strategic choices are recorded; nothing important is only in their head.

---

## Weekly Review (30–45 minutes)

**Intent:** Answer *“Are we working on the right things this week?”*

**Cadence:** Same day each week (Friday afternoon or Monday morning—founder chooses once; BOS remembers).

### Agenda BOS should structure

1. **Initiative scoreboard** — Active bets ranked by: investment to date, progress signal, decision debt, days since last founder touch.
2. **Money without story** — Spend or revenue in ERP not yet attributed to an initiative (founder assigns or dismisses with reason).
3. **Decision audit** — Decisions made this week: expected outcome vs early signal (not full post-mortem yet).
4. **Kill / pause / double-down candidates** — BOS proposes; founder decides.
5. **Next week’s top 3** — Explicit founder commitment, not a task list.

### Weekly success state

Every active initiative has a clear owner, a recent decision, and a known “why we’re still doing this.”

---

## Monthly Review (60–90 minutes)

**Intent:** Answer *“Which bets deserve more capital, less capital, or death?”*

### Agenda

1. **Portfolio allocation view** — % of strategic budget across ventures and initiatives (actual vs intended).
2. **ROI direction, not precision** — Is each initiative trending toward worth-it, unclear, or not-worth-it?
3. **Learning extraction** — What did closed or paused initiatives teach us this month?
4. **Resource reallocation** — Hire, cut, shift budget, merge initiatives.
5. **Next month’s single strategic theme** — e.g., “Prove Digikinz CAC” or “Pause all new bets.”

### Monthly success state

The founder can explain to a board member or co-founder **where money went, why, and what changes next month**—without opening spreadsheets.

---

## Quarterly Review (Half day)

**Intent:** Answer *“Is this venture still the business we meant to build?”*

### Agenda

1. **Venture health** — Revenue, margin direction, initiative mix, team capacity (high level).
2. **Strategic alignment** — Do active initiatives still match the venture thesis?
3. **Institutional memory** — Best decisions, worst decisions, repeated mistakes.
4. **Portfolio pruning** — Kill initiatives that survived on momentum alone.
5. **Next quarter’s initiative slate** — Max 3 new bets; everything else waits.

### Quarterly success state

Fewer, sharper initiatives—with explicit reasons for what was stopped.

---

## Annual Review (1–2 days)

**Intent:** Answer *“What kind of company are we becoming?”*

### Agenda

1. **Venture portfolio** — Which businesses deserve another year of founder energy?
2. **Compounding lessons** — Cross-venture patterns (channels, offers, hiring, pricing).
3. **Capital allocation philosophy** — Update rules: max experiments, max burn per bet, kill thresholds.
4. **Team and operating model** — Who decides what without the founder next year?
5. **Next year’s north star** — One metric or outcome, not twelve OKRs.

### Annual success state

The founder starts the new year with **fewer open loops, clearer rules, and a BOS that remembers what last year taught them.**

---

## Workflow Summary

| Rhythm | Time | Core question | BOS job |
|--------|------|---------------|---------|
| Morning | 5–15 min | What needs me today? | Filter + prioritize |
| Afternoon | 2–5 min | Is today’s bet still valid? | Interrupt only when material |
| Evening | 5–10 min | What closes today? | Capture decisions + blockers |
| Weekly | 30–45 min | Right things this week? | Scoreboard + reallocation |
| Monthly | 60–90 min | Right capital allocation? | Portfolio judgment |
| Quarterly | Half day | Right venture strategy? | Prune + recommit |
| Annual | 1–2 days | Right company? | Memory + rules for next year |

---

## Product North Star for Workflow

If we get this right, the founder’s relationship with BOS feels like:

- **Morning:** Opening a well-edited briefing, not an empty tool
- **Day:** BOS stays quiet unless something important shifts
- **Week+:** BOS becomes the place where judgment compounds

The worst failure mode is BOS becoming **another dashboard they feel guilty for not checking.**

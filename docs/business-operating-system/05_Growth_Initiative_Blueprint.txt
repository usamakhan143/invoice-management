BUSINESS OPERATING SYSTEM (BOS)
Document 05 — Growth Initiative Blueprint
================================================================================

Authoring perspective: Principal ERP Architect · Business Systems Consultant
Status: Business blueprint — pre-implementation
Version: 1.0

The Growth Initiative is the central strategic unit of the entire BOS.
This document defines its complete business design.

================================================================================
1. PURPOSE
================================================================================

A Growth Initiative represents a deliberate, time-bound, measurable effort
to achieve a specific business outcome within a specific business venture.

It is the answer to: "What am I trying to accomplish, and is it working?"

Examples:

    • "Launch Mobile App Agency — Acquire First 10 Clients (Q1–Q2 2026)"
    • "Validate AI Automation SaaS — 100 Signups in 60 Days"
    • "Test Cold Email as Primary Channel for Agency Leads"
    • "Build Personal Brand to Support All Ventures — Content Strategy 2026"
    • "Enter European Market for Mobile App Agency"

An initiative is NOT:
    • A project (delivery work for a specific client)
    • A campaign (a single marketing push)
    • An expense category
    • A task or to-do item

An initiative IS:
    • A strategic bet with expected return
    • A container for investments, channels, and experiments
    • A unit of measurement for ROI
    • A learning vehicle


================================================================================
2. INITIATIVE STRUCTURE (CONCEPTUAL FIELDS)
================================================================================

Identity:
    • Name                    — Human-readable title
    • Business                — Which venture this belongs to
    • Description             — What we are trying to achieve and why
    • Owner                   — Who is responsible (founder, team member)

Timeline:
    • Start date              — When the initiative begins
    • Target end date         — When we expect to evaluate results
    • Actual end date         — When it actually closed (if closed)

Strategy:
    • Strategic goal          — Which OKR/goal this supports
    • Primary channel         — Main acquisition method
    • Secondary channels      — Supporting acquisition methods
    • Cost center(s)          — Where spend is categorized
    • Hypothesis              — "We believe that [action] will result in [outcome]
                               because [reason]"

Financial:
    • Budget allocated        — Planned investment ceiling
    • Investment to date      — Computed from attributed expenses (read-only)
    • Revenue to date         — Computed from attributed invoices (read-only)
    • ROI to date             — Computed (read-only)
    • Target ROI              — Expected return (e.g., 300%)

Measurement:
    • Success criteria        — How we define success (specific, measurable)
    • KPI targets             — Target values for key metrics
    • Current KPI values      — Computed (read-only)


================================================================================
3. LIFECYCLE & STATES
================================================================================

Every initiative moves through a defined lifecycle:

    ┌──────────┐     ┌──────────┐     ┌──────────┐     ┌──────────┐
    │  DRAFT   │────►│  ACTIVE  │────►│  PAUSED  │────►│  CLOSED  │
    └──────────┘     └────┬─────┘     └────┬─────┘     └──────────┘
                          │                │
                          │                └──────► ACTIVE (resume)
                          │
                          └──────────────► CLOSED (kill early)

State definitions:

    DRAFT
        Initiative is being planned. No investments attributed yet.
        Actions: Edit freely, define hypothesis, set budget, assign channels.
        Exit: Activate when ready to begin spending/acquiring.

    ACTIVE
        Initiative is live. Investments, leads, and revenue are being tracked.
        Actions: Run experiments, attribute expenses, monitor KPIs, log decisions.
        Exit: Pause (temporary halt) or Close (final evaluation).

    PAUSED
        Initiative is temporarily halted. No new investments should be attributed.
        Existing data remains. Useful when waiting for market conditions,
        budget approval, or external dependencies.
        Exit: Resume (back to Active) or Close.

    CLOSED
        Initiative is complete. Final ROI calculated. Lessons learned required.
        Sub-states:
            • SUCCESS    — Met or exceeded success criteria
            • PARTIAL    — Some progress but did not meet criteria
            • FAILED     — Did not achieve meaningful results
            • KILLED     — Deliberately stopped before completion
            • PIVOTED    — Changed direction; original initiative closed,
                           new initiative created

Closure requirements:
    • Final ROI computed automatically
    • Lessons learned entry required (cannot close without it)
    • Decision log final entry: "Why are we closing this initiative?"
    • All open experiments must be closed first


================================================================================
4. KEY PERFORMANCE INDICATORS (INITIATIVE-LEVEL)
================================================================================

Each initiative tracks these KPIs automatically (computed, not manually entered):

Investment KPIs:
    • Total investment          — Sum of all attributed expenses + time
    • Budget utilization %      — Investment / Budget allocated
    • Burn rate                 — Investment per week/month
    • Cost per channel          — Investment broken down by acquisition channel

Acquisition KPIs:
    • Total leads generated     — Attributed leads count
    • Cost per lead (CPL)       — Investment / Leads
    • Lead source breakdown     — Leads by channel

Conversion KPIs:
    • Meetings held             — Leads that reached meeting stage
    • Meeting rate              — Meetings / Leads
    • Proposals sent            — Leads that received proposals
    • Proposal rate             — Proposals / Meetings
    • Clients closed            — Leads converted to clients
    • Close rate                — Clients / Proposals
    • Overall conversion rate   — Clients / Leads

Revenue KPIs:
    • Total revenue             — Sum of attributed invoice amounts
    • Average deal size         — Revenue / Clients closed
    • Revenue per channel       — Revenue broken down by channel

ROI KPIs:
    • Gross ROI                 — (Revenue - Investment) / Investment × 100
    • Net ROI                   — (Revenue - Investment - Returns) / Investment
    • ROI per channel           — Channel-specific ROI
    • Time to first revenue     — Days from start to first attributed invoice
    • Time to positive ROI      — Days from start until ROI > 0%

Efficiency KPIs:
    • Sales cycle length        — Average days from lead to client
    • Investment efficiency     — Revenue per dollar invested
    • Channel efficiency ranking — Channels sorted by ROI


================================================================================
5. INVESTMENT MODEL
================================================================================

Investments flow INTO an initiative through the Attribution Engine.
The initiative never stores investment amounts directly — it computes them.

Investment sources:

    Source              | How It Enters                    | Example
    --------------------|----------------------------------|---------------------------
    Expenses            | Post-hoc or inline attribution   | $500 Google Ads expense
    Expense returns     | Reduces net investment           | $50 ad credit refund
    Time (future)       | Manual or integrated time entry  | 20 hours founder time
    Tools/subscriptions | Expense attribution              | $99/month CRM tool
    Contractor payments | Expense attribution              | $2,000 developer payment
    Ad platform spend   | Adapter or manual expense        | $1,200 Meta Ads

Investment rules:
    • One expense can be split across multiple initiatives (allocation %)
    • One expense can be attributed to initiative + channel + experiment simultaneously
    • Unattributed expenses are visible but do not count toward any initiative ROI
    • Returns/refunds reduce net investment automatically


================================================================================
6. ROI MODEL
================================================================================

ROI is the single most important output of an initiative.

    Gross ROI  = (Total Attributed Revenue - Total Investment) / Total Investment × 100

    Net ROI    = (Total Attributed Revenue - Total Investment + Total Returns) / Total Investment × 100

    Channel ROI = (Channel Revenue - Channel Investment) / Channel Investment × 100

ROI interpretation guidelines:

    ROI > 300%     — Excellent. Scale investment.
    ROI 100–300%   — Good. Continue and optimize.
    ROI 0–100%     — Marginal. Evaluate channel mix.
    ROI < 0%       — Losing money. Decide: pivot, pause, or kill.
    ROI = N/A      — No revenue yet. Evaluate leading indicators (leads, meetings).

ROI is computed in real-time as new expenses and invoices are attributed.
It is never manually entered.


================================================================================
7. REVENUE ATTRIBUTION
================================================================================

Revenue enters an initiative when:

    1. A lead attributed to this initiative converts to a client
    2. An invoice is created for that client
    3. The invoice is linked (via attribution) to the initiative

Attribution models (in order of sophistication):

    Phase 1 — Last Touch:
        Revenue attributed to the initiative that generated the converting lead.

    Phase 2 — First Touch:
        Revenue attributed to the initiative that first acquired the lead.

    Phase 3 — Multi-Touch (future):
        Revenue split across all initiatives that touched the lead journey.

Start with Last Touch. It is simple, auditable, and sufficient for
single-founder operations.

8. DECISION FRAMEWORK
================================================================================

The BOS should recommend decisions based on initiative performance.
These are not automated actions — they are recommendations for the founder.

Decision triggers:

    ┌─────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────────────┐
    │ Signal                  │ Recommended Decision                       │
    ├─────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────────┤
    │ ROI > target after 90d  │ SCALE — increase budget, expand channels   │
    │ ROI positive but low    │ OPTIMIZE — run experiments, cut weak channels│
    │ ROI negative after 60d  │ PIVOT — change channel, offer, or audience │
    │ ROI negative after 90d  │ KILL — close initiative, capture lessons   │
    │ Budget 80% used, no     │ REVIEW — evaluate leading indicators,      │
    │   revenue yet           │   decide continue/pause/kill               │
    │ One channel ROI 3x      │ REALLOCATE — shift budget to winning channel│
    │   others                │                                            │
    │ Experiment success      │ ADOPT — roll winning variant into main     │
    │ Experiment failure      │ ARCHIVE — log lesson, try next experiment  │
    └─────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────────────────┘

Every decision is logged in the Decision Log with rationale.


================================================================================
9. EXPERIMENTS WITHIN INITIATIVES
================================================================================

Experiments are structured tests nested inside an initiative.

    Initiative: "Launch Mobile App Agency — Client Acquisition"
    ├── Experiment 1: "LinkedIn outreach — case study vs no case study"
    │   Hypothesis: "Leads with case study convert 2x"
    │   Budget: $200 (tool costs)
    │   Duration: 2 weeks
    │   Success: Meeting rate > 10%
    │   Result: SUCCESS — 14% meeting rate with case study
    │
    ├── Experiment 2: "Google Ads — service keywords vs problem keywords"
    │   Hypothesis: "Problem keywords have lower CPL"
    │   Budget: $500
    │   Duration: 1 month
    │   Success: CPL < $15
    │   Result: FAILED — CPL was $42, lesson captured
    │
    └── Experiment 3: "Cold email — 3 subject line variants"
        Status: ACTIVE

Experiment lifecycle: PLANNED → ACTIVE → COMPLETED (success/failure/inconclusive)
Each experiment closure feeds into the initiative's Lessons Learned.


================================================================================
10. LESSONS LEARNED (INITIATIVE CLOSURE)
================================================================================

When an initiative closes, a structured lesson capture is required:

    ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
    │  LESSON LEARNED — Initiative Closure                     │
    ├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
    │  Business:        Mobile App Agency                      │
    │  Initiative:      Q1 Client Acquisition                 │
    │  Outcome:         SUCCESS (ROI 775%)                     │
    │  Duration:        92 days                                │
    │  Investment:      $3,200                                 │
    │  Revenue:         $28,000                                │
    │                                                         │
    │  What worked:                                           │
    │  • LinkedIn outreach with case studies (14% meeting rate)│
    │  • Targeting CTOs at 10-50 person companies              │
    │  • $2,500-$5,000 project range sweet spot               │
    │                                                         │
    │  What didn't work:                                      │
    │  • Google Ads ($42 CPL — too expensive for agency model)│
    │  • Facebook Ads (zero qualified leads)                   │
    │                                                         │
    │  Key insight:                                           │
    │  Direct outreach to technical decision-makers with proof │
    │  of past work is the highest-ROI channel for agencies.   │
    │                                                         │
    │  Recommendation for future:                             │
    │  Start with LinkedIn outreach for any new agency launch. │
    │  Skip paid ads until organic channel is validated.       │
    │                                                         │
    │  Tags: [agency] [linkedin] [outreach] [success]          │
    └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

This lesson becomes searchable and surfaces when launching similar initiatives.

11. FUTURE EXPANSION
================================================================================

Phase 1 (Year 1):
    Manual initiative creation, post-hoc attribution, basic KPI computation

Phase 2 (Year 2):
    Initiative templates/playbooks, automated KPI dashboards, decision triggers

Phase 3 (Year 3):
    Cross-initiative comparison, channel benchmarks, AI-powered recommendations

Phase 4 (Year 5):
    Multi-business portfolio view, automated resource allocation suggestions,
    predictive ROI modeling based on historical data

Phase 5 (Year 10):
    Semi-autonomous initiative management — system proposes initiatives based
    on market signals, historical patterns, and portfolio gaps

================================================================================
BUSINESS OPERATING SYSTEM (BOS)
Document 07 — Data Flow
================================================================================

Authoring perspective: Principal ERP Architect · Business Systems Consultant
Status: Business blueprint — pre-implementation
Version: 1.0

This document traces how information flows through the entire BOS —
from the first dollar invested to the final lesson learned.

================================================================================
1. MASTER DATA FLOW
================================================================================

    Investment ──► Campaign ──► Lead ──► Meeting ──► Proposal ──►
    Client ──► Revenue ──► ROI ──► Lessons Learned

Each step is explained in detail below.


================================================================================
2. STEP 1 — INVESTMENT
================================================================================

What happens:
    The founder decides to invest money, time, or resources into a business
    initiative. This is the starting point of every business effort.

Business question answered:
    "Where am I investing, and how much?"

Flow:

    Founder Decision
        │
        ▼
    Growth Initiative created (DRAFT → ACTIVE)
        │
        ├── Budget allocated: $5,000
        ├── Channel selected: LinkedIn Outreach
        ├── Hypothesis defined: "CTOs at 10-50 person companies will respond
        │   to case study-based outreach"
        └── Success criteria set: 10 clients in 90 days
        │
        ▼
    Money is spent (operational layer — unchanged):
        │
        ├── Expense: $500 — LinkedIn Sales Navigator (tool)
        │   └── BOS Attribution: Initiative X, Channel: LinkedIn, Cost Center: Tools
        │
        ├── Expense: $200 — Email verification service
        │   └── BOS Attribution: Initiative X, Channel: LinkedIn, Experiment #1
        │
        ├── Expense: $1,500 — Contractor for outreach copywriting
        │   └── BOS Attribution: Initiative X, Channel: LinkedIn
        │
        └── Expense: $800 — Google Ads (testing secondary channel)
            └── BOS Attribution: Initiative X, Channel: Google Ads, Experiment #2

Data captured:
    • Initiative record (BOS — owned)
    • Expense records (ERP — owned, unchanged)
    • Attribution links (BOS — owned)
    • Running investment total (BOS — computed)

Key metrics at this stage:
    • Total investment to date
    • Budget utilization %
    • Burn rate (investment per week)
    • Investment by channel breakdown


================================================================================
3. STEP 2 — CAMPAIGN / EXPERIMENT
================================================================================

What happens:
    Based on the initiative strategy, specific campaigns and experiments are
    launched to test channels and approaches.

Business question answered:
    "What specific actions are we taking to acquire customers?"

Flow:

    Initiative (ACTIVE)
        │
        ├── Experiment #1: "LinkedIn — case study vs no case study"
        │   Status: ACTIVE
        │   Budget: $200
        │   Duration: 2 weeks
        │   Linked to: CRM Campaign "Agency Q1 Outreach"
        │
        ├── Experiment #2: "Google Ads — service vs problem keywords"
        │   Status: ACTIVE
        │   Budget: $800
        │   Duration: 4 weeks
        │
        └── CRM Campaign: "Agency Q1 Outreach"
            (Existing CRM module — unchanged)
            Linked to BOS Initiative X via attribution

Data captured:
    • Experiment records (BOS — owned)
    • CRM campaign records (CRM — owned, linked via BOS)
    • Campaign-attributed expenses (via BOS attribution)
    • External platform data (via adapters, if connected)

Key metrics at this stage:
    • Experiment progress (days elapsed vs planned)
    • Experiment spend vs budget
    • Campaign activity volume (emails sent, ads shown)


================================================================================
4. STEP 3 — LEAD GENERATED
================================================================================

What happens:
    A potential customer enters the system — through outreach response, ad click,
    referral, website form, or any acquisition channel.

Business question answered:
    "Is our investment producing potential customers?"

Flow:

    Campaign/Experiment activity
        │
        ▼
    Lead enters CRM (operational layer — unchanged):
        │
        ├── CRM Lead created: "John Smith, CTO at TechCo"
        │   Source: LinkedIn outreach response
        │   Campaign: "Agency Q1 Outreach"
        │
        └── BOS Attribution created:
            • Lead → Initiative X
            • Lead → Channel: LinkedIn
            • Lead → Experiment #1

    Alternative entry paths:
        │
        ├── GoHighLevel funnel → GHL Adapter → BOS Attribution
        ├── Google Ads click → Landing page form → CRM Lead → BOS Attribution
        ├── Referral → Manual CRM entry → BOS Attribution
        └── Cold email reply → CRM Lead → BOS Attribution

Data captured:
    • Lead record (CRM — owned)
    • BOS attribution (BOS — owned)
    • Lead source and channel (CRM + BOS)
    • Timestamp (for funnel velocity tracking)

Key metrics at this stage:
    • Total leads generated
    • Leads by channel
    • Cost per lead (CPL) = Investment / Leads
    • Lead generation rate (leads per week)
    • Channel comparison (which channel produces most leads at lowest CPL)


================================================================================
5. STEP 4 — MEETING HELD
================================================================================

What happens:
    A meaningful conversation occurs between the founder/team and the lead.
    This is the first qualification gate in the funnel.

Business question answered:
    "Are the leads we're generating actually interested?"

Flow:

    Lead (in CRM)
        │
        ▼
    Meeting occurs:
        │
        ├── CRM Activity logged: "Discovery call with John Smith — 30 min"
        │   (Existing call activity module — unchanged)
        │
        └── BOS Funnel Event recorded:
            • Lead ID → Stage: MEETING HELD
            • Timestamp
            • Initiative X, Channel: LinkedIn

Data captured:
    • Call/activity record (CRM — owned)
    • Funnel stage transition (BOS — owned)
    • Meeting date (for sales cycle calculation)

Key metrics at this stage:
    • Meetings held (total)
    • Meeting rate = Meetings / Leads × 100
    • Meeting rate by channel
    • Cost per meeting = Investment / Meetings
    • Average time from lead to meeting (days)


================================================================================
6. STEP 5 — PROPOSAL SENT
================================================================================

What happens:
    A formal proposal, quote, or scope of work is sent to the qualified lead.
    This represents serious buying intent.

Business question answered:
    "How many interested leads are we converting to opportunities?"

Flow:

    Meeting held (qualified lead)
        │
        ▼
    Proposal sent:
        │
        ├── CRM status updated: "Proposal Sent"
        │   (Or custom stage tracked in BOS)
        │
        └── BOS Funnel Event recorded:
            • Lead ID → Stage: PROPOSAL SENT
            • Proposal amount: $4,500
            • Timestamp

Data captured:
    • CRM status change (CRM — owned)
    • Funnel stage transition (BOS — owned)
    • Proposal amount (for pipeline value tracking)

Key metrics at this stage:
    • Proposals sent (total)
    • Proposal rate = Proposals / Meetings × 100
    • Average proposal value
    • Pipeline value (sum of open proposal amounts)
    • Proposal rate by channel


================================================================================
7. STEP 6 — CLIENT CLOSED
================================================================================

What happens:
    The lead accepts the proposal and becomes a paying client. This is the
    primary conversion event in the funnel.

Business question answered:
    "Are we actually winning business from our investment?"

Flow:

    Proposal accepted
        │
        ▼
    Client conversion:
        │
        ├── CRM: Lead status → Converted
        │   Customer/Business record created (existing CRM flow)
        │
        ├── BOS Funnel Event: Stage → CLIENT CLOSED
        │
        └── BOS Revenue Attribution prepared:
            (Revenue will be counted when invoice is paid)

Data captured:
    • Conversion record (CRM — owned)
    • Funnel stage transition (BOS — owned)
    • Deal size (proposal amount or negotiated amount)
    • Sales cycle length = Client close date - Lead date

Key metrics at this stage:
    • Clients closed (total)
    • Close rate = Clients / Proposals × 100
    • Overall conversion rate = Clients / Leads × 100
    • Average deal size
    • Sales cycle length (average days)
    • Close rate by channel
    • Cost per acquisition (CAC) = Investment / Clients


================================================================================
8. STEP 7 — REVENUE RECORDED
================================================================================

What happens:
    Money is received from the client — via invoice payment, direct deposit,
    or any revenue mechanism.

Business question answered:
    "How much money did this initiative actually produce?"

Flow:

    Client closed
        │
        ▼
    Revenue enters ERP (operational layer — unchanged):
        │
        ├── Invoice created: $4,500 — "Mobile App Development for TechCo"
        │   Customer linked to converted lead
        │
        ├── Invoice paid → Bank account credited
        │
        └── BOS Revenue Attribution:
            • Invoice → Initiative X
            • Invoice → Channel: LinkedIn
            • Revenue amount: $4,500

    Multiple revenue events per initiative:
        │
        ├── Invoice #1: $4,500 (TechCo — project)
        ├── Invoice #2: $8,000 (StartupY — project)
        ├── Invoice #3: $3,200 (CorpZ — project)
        └── Invoice #4: $12,000 (BigCo — project)
        │
        └── Total attributed revenue: $27,700

Data captured:
    • Invoice record (ERP — owned)
    • Payment record (ERP — owned)
    • Revenue attribution (BOS — owned)
    • Time to first revenue (days from initiative start)

Key metrics at this stage:
    • Total revenue (attributed to initiative)
    • Revenue by channel
    • Average deal size
    • Revenue per dollar invested
    • Time to first revenue
    • Outstanding pipeline (proposals not yet invoiced)


================================================================================
9. STEP 8 — ROI COMPUTED
================================================================================

What happens:
    The BOS automatically computes return on investment by comparing total
    attributed revenue against total attributed investment.

Business question answered:
    "Was this initiative worth the investment?"

Flow:

    All attributed data collected
        │
        ▼
    KPI Engine computes:

        Initiative: "Launch Mobile App Agency — Q1 Client Acquisition"
        ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
        Investment:     $3,200
        Returns:        $50 (ad credit refund)
        Net Investment: $3,150
        Revenue:        $27,700
        ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
        Gross ROI:      765%  = ($27,700 - $3,200) / $3,200 × 100
        Net ROI:        779%  = ($27,700 - $3,150) / $3,150 × 100
        ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
        By Channel:
        • LinkedIn:  Investment $1,700 → Revenue $22,500 → ROI 1,224%
        • Google Ads: Investment $800 → Revenue $5,200 → ROI 550%
        • Referral:  Investment $0 → Revenue $0 → N/A

        Decision Recommendation: SCALE — ROI exceeds 300% target.
        Suggested action: Increase LinkedIn budget, reduce Google Ads.

Data captured:
    • All KPI values (BOS — computed, cached for performance)
    • ROI by initiative, channel, experiment (BOS — computed)
    • Decision recommendation (BOS — generated)
    • Timestamp of computation

Key metrics at this stage:
    • ROI (gross and net)
    • ROI by channel
    • ROI trend over time
    • Comparison to target ROI
    • Comparison to other initiatives
    • Comparison to other businesses in portfolio


================================================================================
10. STEP 9 — LESSONS LEARNED
================================================================================

What happens:
    The initiative is closed (success, failure, or pivot) and the founder
    captures structured lessons for future reference.

Business question answered:
    "What did we learn, and what should we do differently next time?"

Flow:

    Initiative CLOSED (outcome: SUCCESS)
        │
        ▼
    Required: Lessons Learned entry
        │
        ├── What worked (specific, actionable)
        ├── What didn't work (specific, actionable)
        ├── Key insight (the "so what")
        ├── Recommendation (for future initiatives)
        ├── Tags (searchable: business type, channel, outcome)
        │
        ▼
    Decision Log final entry:
        "Closing initiative with SUCCESS outcome. ROI 779% exceeded 300% target.
         Recommending LinkedIn outreach as primary channel for future agency launches."
        │
        ▼
    Optional: Create Playbook
        "Agency Launch Playbook v1" derived from this initiative's data
        │
        ▼
    Future initiative creation:
        When founder creates "Launch AI Automation Agency — Q1 2027":
        BOS surfaces: "Similar initiative found: Mobile App Agency Q1 2026.
        Key lesson: Start with LinkedIn outreach. Avoid Google Ads until
        organic channel validated. Expected CPL: $12-15."

Data captured:
    • Lesson learned record (BOS — owned)
    • Decision log entry (BOS — owned)
    • Playbook template (BOS — owned, optional)
    • Initiative closure record with final KPIs (BOS — owned)

Key metrics at this stage:
    • Lesson count by business type
    • Success rate by channel (across all initiatives)
    • Playbook usage count
    • Institutional knowledge growth over time

12. DATA FLOW RULES
================================================================================

    1. Data always flows FORWARD through the funnel (Investment → Lesson)
    2. Each step can happen independently (not every lead needs a meeting)
    3. Steps can be skipped (referral lead may go straight to client)
    4. Multiple paths can feed one initiative (multiple channels)
    5. One expense can feed multiple initiatives (split attribution)
    6. ROI is always computed, never manually entered
    7. Lessons learned are always captured on closure, never optional
    8. External systems feed into the flow via adapters, not direct integration
